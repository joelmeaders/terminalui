# License guidance

Terminal Kit uses the custom [Terminal Kit Free Distribution License 1.0](../LICENSE). The license file controls. This guide explains the intended boundary and release status.

The draft is ready for legal review. It is not a standard license or a claim of legal approval. The package remains private and unpublished.

## Intended boundary

The library, its distributed modifications, and its derived components and themes must remain free to redistribute. People may use them in paid applications. They may also distribute free competing forks.

| Example                                                                        | Intended result                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A paid game uses Terminal Kit for its menus.                                   | Allowed. The game supplies independent functionality.                       |
| A subscription website uses Terminal Kit for its interface.                    | Allowed. The subscription pays for the application.                         |
| A client pays a developer to build a business application.                     | Allowed. The fee pays for implementation work.                              |
| A free fork replaces or extends the kit.                                       | Allowed under the same terms, with notices and modification identification. |
| A derived component or theme pack is shared without charge.                    | Allowed under the same terms.                                               |
| A kit fork, derived component pack, or derived theme is sold.                  | Prohibited. The reusable Covered Material is the product.                   |
| A component pack requires a paid membership or bundle purchase.                | Prohibited. Renaming the access fee does not remove it.                     |
| Optional donations accompany a free download.                                  | Allowed. A donation cannot be required.                                     |
| Paid maintenance or training accompanies an independently accessible free kit. | Allowed. Access and reuse cannot require the service purchase.              |

Application code can use separate terms and remain private. The license does not require publication of the application or unshared private modifications. Distributed library-derived material keeps its own license, even inside a paid application. The application's terms must preserve that exception.

The draft covers material that the copyright holders can license. Third-party icons and dependencies keep their upstream terms. Independently written themes or extensions that contain no Covered Material retain their own rights. Compatibility alone does not make their copyright belong to Terminal Kit.

## Continuing free rights

The draft grants continuing, royalty-free permissions for versions offered under these terms, while recipients comply. Later policy changes do not withdraw those grants. Distributed derivatives carry the same terms. A distributor cannot replace them with a paid toolkit license.

Project policy keeps future Terminal Kit releases under these terms and offers no paid toolkit license exception. This policy does not change earlier license grants or guarantee another party's compliance. Legal review must assess how to document the copyright holders' continuing commitments and contributor permissions.

Earlier repository versions used PolyForm Perimeter. This change does not retroactively cancel grants made under that license. Retain the license associated with any older copy.

## Why these are custom terms

[PolyForm Perimeter 1.0.1](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/perimeter/1.0.1) restricts competing products, including free alternatives. That restriction conflicts with the permitted free forks here.

The [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd) requires freedom to sell redistributed software. A no-sale condition makes these source-available terms, even though paid applications may use the library.

## Before public release

Legal review must confirm the application/toolkit distinction, service-fee boundary, same-term derivative obligations, patent grant, and continuing permissions. Confirm ownership and contributor consent for the material offered under the custom terms. Review independent themes, third-party licenses, and prior grants separately.

The installed-package check verifies that the tarball contains the exact repository license and upstream notices. It does not assess legal enforceability. Publication still needs explicit owner authorization.
